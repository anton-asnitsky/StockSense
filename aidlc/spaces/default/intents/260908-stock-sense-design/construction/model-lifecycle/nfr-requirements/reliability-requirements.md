# Model Lifecycle Reliability Requirements

Unit: U6 Model Lifecycle (`model-lifecycle`)

## Requirements

| ID | Requirement | Acceptance and evidence | Failure behavior |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| NFR7.1 | RabbitMQ integration shall use publisher confirms, transactional outbox/inbox, acknowledgement after local commit, and tenant-scoped operation keys plus canonical request hashes. A 60-second execution lease renews every 15 seconds; four missed heartbeats fence the attempt. Each logical job allows at most three lease-acquiring attempts with 30-second-to-five-minute exponential backoff plus jitter. Queue capacity expiry follows NFR2.2, creates no attempt, never retries automatically, and is not a resource-limit execution failure. | Interrupt API, outbox relay, broker, worker, object upload, MLflow write, control-store commit, heartbeat, and acknowledgement around every boundary; prove exact replay, changed-payload conflict, fencing, checkpoint verification, attempt-budget accounting, the separate capacity-expiry path, and no duplicate route change. | Retry only transient dependency/worker-loss/verified-checkpoint failures after a failed or interrupted attempt. Data, schema, leakage, checksum, authorization, and execution resource-limit failures are terminal. Capacity-expired callers may only create the separately authorized new logical command defined by NFR2.2. |
| NFR9.1 | Dependencies override retention. After references end, superseded model bytes remain 90 days; rejected-candidate and failed-attempt bytes 30 days; dataset/model bytes and canonical provenance remain in encrypted archive one year; business audit remains 90 days, logs seven days, and recovery snapshots 30 days. Dead letters remain seven days and replay batches contain at most 20 jobs. | Time-travel, dependency, expiry, rebuild, rollback, and replay tests prove active routes, retained forecasts, promotions, restore sets, and audit evidence block deletion; tombstones prevent reappearance. | Retain on uncertainty, keep failed expiry visible, and never reactivate expired bytes or erase identity/checksum history. |
| NFR11.1 | A clean reviewer shall download pinned CPU-compatible prerequisites, publish the seeded dataset, train both baselines and the real HistGradientBoosting candidate, run six-origin evaluation three times, inspect MLflow, validate/promote/rollback a release, and resolve its artifact without owner credentials, cached models, cloud services, or GPU. | Evidence records versions, terms, checksums, commands, hardware, durations, resources, forecasts/metrics, selected and rejected candidates, route changes, and limitations. | Mark reproducibility failed; fixtures-only results or owner-machine/GPU evidence cannot substitute. |
| NFR13.2 | Flyway/routine incompatibility, MLflow schema failure, incompatible manifests, model/runtime/schema mismatch, or failed contract compatibility shall block rollout or release activation. | Upgrade and rollback tests use immutable application versions and compatible schemas; routine grants and migration ordering are verified. | Stop rollout/activation and preserve the last compatible deployment and route. |
| NFR15.1 | A checksummed recovery manifest shall cover PostgreSQL lifecycle/control records, MLflow metadata, and immutable object snapshots with RPO 24 hours and RTO two hours. Restore all authorities unavailable, then reconcile tenant ownership, datasets, runs, evaluations, artifacts, checksums, runtime/schema compatibility, history, retention, and routes per retailer before use. | Every tagged portfolio release runs a clean-target drill and records elapsed time, data loss, manifest/checksum results, unavailable interval, discrepancies, and route activation. | Keep affected routes unavailable, preserve discrepancy evidence, and require explicit Operator selection of a validated fallback; never choose the newest artifact silently. |

## Degraded behavior

MLflow outage blocks new evidence-dependent validation but cannot alter active
routes. Object loss makes affected releases unavailable. PostgreSQL authority
loss rejects lifecycle mutations and route resolution. Telemetry/OpenSearch
outage does not block authoritative commits but raises degraded observability.
